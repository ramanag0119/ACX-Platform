
HMS v3 Architecture Summary
OSS (PostgreSQL) + BSS (MySQL) Integration
Architecture, Ownership, Integration Rules & v3 Improvements

Executive / Team Briefing Document

1. Executive Summary
Core principle: Two databases, one platform. OSS (PostgreSQL) owns the building and its devices, while BSS (MySQL) owns the hotel business. HMS is the business layer/module sitting on top of FleetExplorer, not a separate parallel platform.
2. Architecture at a Glance
Layer 3 — HMS Dashboard / Staff UI
Reads required business and platform information through APIs.
OSS — PostgreSQL 16 + TimescaleDB
Owns the physical building, users, permissions, devices, telemetry, MQTT and access events.
BSS — MySQL 8.0
Owns hotel business operations such as rooms, bookings, stays, guests, revenue, services, channels, loyalty and marketing.
The OSS and BSS systems communicate through application/API-level references. The database boundary is intentionally preserved.

6. Five Critical Architecture Rules
Rule 1 — One user identity, one platform
oss_app_user is the single user record for hotel staff, guests and admins. bss_users is deprecated and will be removed after migration. is_hms_staff = 1 identifies HMS staff.
Rule 2 — OSS owns telemetry, BSS never duplicates it
All sensor readings remain in oss_device_stat as a TimescaleDB hypertable. BSS keeps only a health snapshot in bss_room_device_registry.last_known_health_status.
Rule 3 — BSS owns the stay, OSS provides IoT context
bss_stays is the authoritative check-in record with planned_check_in, planned_check_out, actual_check_in and actual_check_out. oss_stay is a lightweight IoT context record.
Rule 4 — RBAC is module-aware
Every oss_role carries a module_id. HMS roles cannot automatically access FleetExplorer pages. platform_modules provides module-level separation.
Rule 5 — Device alerts are clickable links, not data copies
HMS dashboard alert counts should link to the corresponding FleetExplorer alert. bss_room_conditions.oss_device_alert_id acts as the reference.

8. Recommended Team / Kumar Briefing Flow
Stage
Message
Opening — 30 seconds
The v3 schema enforces the core principle: OSS is the platform and HMS is a module on top of it. Users, devices and telemetry remain in FleetExplorer/PostgreSQL; MySQL stores hotel-specific business data.
Boundary — 1 minute
Cross-system references are application-level references, not database foreign keys. HMS should use APIs when it needs device/platform information and should not directly read PostgreSQL.
Fixes — 2 minutes
Highlight the v2 fixes: deprecated bss_users references were replaced by oss_app_user; housekeeping assignment now supports the unified user; device health snapshots provide a reliable dashboard source.
Close — 1 minute
Emphasize bss_automation_events for auditable checkout-to-housekeeping automation and bss_events for banquet/conference bookings with cancel-only lifecycle.
9. Final Takeaway
HMS v3 is designed around clear ownership: OSS owns the platform, physical building, identity, permissions, devices and telemetry; BSS owns the hotel business, including rooms, bookings, stays, guests, revenue and services. The API/application layer is the controlled bridge between the two systems. This separation prevents duplicate ownership while allowing HMS to deliver a unified business dashboard.
